So what!

atheism

Given that the universe operates according to fixed natural laws, one might question whether belief in God holds any real significance. However, if we accept the existence of a Creator, as previously established, it follows that what happens next is not for us to decide but rests entirely with this Creator.

If the Creator had the power to bring us into existence, it is reasonable to conclude that He could recreate us at any time. Interestingly, advancements in genetics and technology suggest that humans may one day achieve feats like transferring their minds into younger replicas derived from their genetic code. If such possibilities exist within human imagination and ingenuity, how much more plausible is it that the original Creator could gather all the necessary components — both material and immaterial — to recreate us entirely?

Freedom, Responsibility, and the Afterlife

God does not interfere in human affairs, as humans have been granted the freedom of choice. However, this freedom comes with the responsibility to build a society rooted in compassion and mutual care. A selfless life paves the way for a harmonious existence — a paradise — while selfish desires create a hellish existence for those consumed by greed.

The paradise- and hell-like societies we experience are the collective outcomes of our actions in this life. Similarly, individual fates await us in the afterlife, determined by the choices we make during our lifetime. The paths we choose shape our ultimate destination, whether it be a paradise or a hell-like existence.

This discussion does not revolve around “irrational beliefs” or “meaningless rituals.” Instead, it underscores a practical and grounded approach to life — one that enhances our collective well-being on Earth and prepares us for the second stage of existence.

Death Is a Transition Portal

Consider the concept of death as a transition portal to a higher-dimensional realm, akin to the depiction in the movie Avatar. In this realm, our consciousness is housed in new “avatar” bodies specifically suited to this environment.

These avatars begin as nascent and underdeveloped, requiring nourishment to grow and thrive. Their nourishment is linked to a metaphysical system that reflects our earthly actions. Moral and virtuous deeds provide the nourishment needed to strengthen the avatars, while selfish or harmful actions lead to their malnourishment. Just as physical laws govern our bodies in this world, moral laws shape the development of our avatars in the higher-dimensional realm.

The ultimate strength and condition of our avatars will determine whether we can pass through the gates of heaven to enjoy its rewards or whether we face the hellfire of regret and remorse. This framework makes clear that the rewards and consequences of the afterlife are solely the result of an individual’s choices, removing any basis for blaming a higher power for perceived injustices.

The Afterlife Dialog: An Allegorical Narrative The Quran presents an allegorical narrative of the afterlife, illustrating the accountability and consequences of our earthly actions - [Quran 36:51-65].

And when the individuals will be resurrected in [the bodies of their afterlife] avatars, they will say, “Woe to us! Who has brought us out from our sleeping pods?” It will be answered, “This is what the Most Merciful had promised; the messengers told the truth!”

“… This Day, no one will be wronged at all, nor will you be recompensed except for what you used to do.” [You have nourished or malnourished your avatars with your own actions. There is no one else to be blamed here but yourself.]

Indeed, on that Day the residents of Paradise will be busy enjoying themselves [in their stronger avatar bodies] …

“Step away this Day O wicked ones!”

The narrative emphasizes that the state of their avatars — either nourished or malnourished — is a direct reflection of their actions on earth. Blame cannot be shifted to anyone else.

The residents of Paradise, in their stronger and fully developed avatars, will enjoy a state of bliss. In contrast, the wicked will be cast away and reminded of their choices:

“Did I not commanded you, O Children of Adam, not to worship Satan [meaning not to follow Satanic selfish desires]? For he is truly your sworn enemy … Yet he has certainly misled a great multitude of you astray [from the true rational path], so did you not use reason [to find the truth?]

The wicked will face the reality of their choices:

This is Hell you were warned of … On this Day, We will seal their [ avatar’s ] mouths [so that they do not fabricate excuses like they used to do on earth], and their hands will speak to Us and their feet will testify to what they used to earn. [The development of their avatars will reflect the nature of their deeds and conduct on earth.]

The development — or lack thereof — of their avatars will stand as undeniable evidence of their earthly conduct.

This allegorical narrative concludes with a reminder:

We have not taught him [the propeht] poetry, nor is it fitting for him [to invent allegorical poety in our Name]. This [ Book ] is only a Reminder and a clear Quran .. to warn whoever is truly alive, and fullfil the decree against the truth deniers!
