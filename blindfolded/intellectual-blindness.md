# Intellectual Blindness

Navigating the complexities and challenges of life is no small feat, and uncovering a universal solution that addresses them is an extraordinary accomplishment. However, the greater challenge lies in communicating this solution to humanity's vast and diverse audience. The variety of languages, cultural nuances, and levels of understanding among people make this task daunting. A one-size-fits-all approach is bound to fall short. To truly connect, one must tailor their language and communication style to resonate with each unique group. Whether speaking to an illiterate individual in a remote village or engaging with a learned scholar in academia, the delivery must align with the recipient's level of understanding and context.

If the Quran is to serve as a timeless source of guidance for all — cutting across barriers of background, education, and culture — it must transcend the limitations of its era. Its language and style must remain as relevant and profound today as they were when first revealed. Such a text would not only address life's intricacies and challenges but also provide enduring solutions that invite reflection and adaptation.

To achieve this, the Quran's language must be captivating, able to hold the attention of its readers even when delving into profound psychological or philosophical concepts. Its approach would naturally blend allegories, symbols, and references to familiar elements, crafting narratives rich in meaning. These stories would not merely inform but also inspire deeper thought and contemplation.

For example, instead of presenting a dry, academic discourse on human behavior and the best courses of action, the Quran could employ vivid metaphors and imaginative analogies to make complex ideas accessible. Consider the metaphor of blindness as seen in The [Country of The Blind](/?Blindfolded&part=1) - an evocative way to represent certain human behaviors and limitations. Such creative storytelling could serve as a powerful tool for conveying profound truths in a relatable and thought-provoking manner.

## Metaphors in the Quran

Metaphors in the Quran serve as powerful tools, weaving vivid illustrations that resonate deeply with readers and transcend the boundaries of time and language.

One such metaphor is '`blindness`', symbolizing a state where individuals abandon reason and logic, allowing desires and emotions to dictate their decisions. This often leads to the unquestioning acceptance of unverified dogmas and baseless beliefs, blinding individuals to the pursuit of truth and enlightenment.

Similarly, the Quran employs '`death`' as a metaphor to represent stagnation and decay, whether in individuals or entire societies. It depicts a condition where, despite physical existence, there is a lack of responsiveness to truth and constructive dialogue—akin to speaking to the lifeless. This metaphor underscores the need for transformative change, urging a shift towards a vibrant, purposeful, and progressive way of life.

The Quran's masterful use of such metaphors enables it to communicate profound truths in a memorable and engaging way. By eschewing overly technical language, it appeals directly to both the intellect and the soul, making its messages accessible to people across diverse backgrounds.

This is articulated clearly in the Quran:

@{q-trans-en} \[Intellectually] blind and the seeing are not equal. Nor are the darknesses and the light ... And the living and the dead are not alike ... @ref{Quran 35:19-22}

Here, the Quran eloquently casts itself as '`the light`' - a luminous guide for those seeking truth and enlightenment. In contrast, everything outside its teachings is portrayed as '`darkness`'. The capacity for reasoning and intellectual inquiry is celebrated as "seeing," while the obstinate clinging to irrational beliefs is described as "blindness." These metaphors emphasize the essential role of reason and critical thinking in the pursuit of knowledge and understanding.

Much like navigating in pitch darkness requires light, true guidance depends on two essential elements: the Quran as the source of enlightenment (the light) and intellectual reasoning (the seeing eyes) as the means to perceive it.

> Just as light holds no value without one's ability to see, the Quran's teachings cannot be fully grasped or applied without a discerning and reflective mind.

## The Blindness

The Quran repeatedly emphasizes the transformative mission of God's messengers: to liberate humanity from the chains of false beliefs and herd mentality. These messengers sought to awaken the innate reasoning power within individuals, empowering them to discern right from wrong through the lens of logic and evidence.

Yet, despite the messengers' tireless efforts to illuminate the path of truth, many individuals resolutely clung to their entrenched traditions and inherited beliefs. They staunchly declared, "We shall not abandon the beliefs of our forefathers, even if they defy reason. Our ancestors followed these beliefs, and so shall we." This defiance highlights the profound grip of inherited beliefs, which often prevail even when faced with compelling evidence of their fallacy.

Consider the story of Prophet Noah, who called upon his people to abandon their fruitless idol worship. Instead of engaging in intellectual inquiry, they held steadfast to their blind faith, offering a response rooted in ancestral allegiance:

@{q-trans-en} (They said,) '... We never heard such (a rational evaluation of faith) from our elders (and sages) of earlier generations.' @ref{Quran 23:23-24}

This phenomenon of intellectual blindness, often anchored in unwavering loyalty to ancestral traditions, transcends religious and cultural boundaries. Time and again, the Quran recounts similar resistance faced by various prophets, illustrating the universal nature of this challenge.

For instance, when Prophet Saleh urged his people to turn away from worshiping hollow deities, their reaction was emblematic of the stubborn adherence to inherited practices:

@{q-trans-en} They said, 'O Saleh! We had placed great hopes in you before this! But how dare you tell us to move away from worshiping what our elders worshiped? ...' @ref{Quran 11:62}

A parallel narrative emerges with Prophet Abraham, who questioned his father and community about their devotion to lifeless statues. Their response, void of reasoning, epitomized intellectual complacency:

@{q-trans-en} When he (Abraham) said to his father and his people, 'What are these images and statues to which you are devoted (Give me some rational reasoning for your behavior)?' They responded, 'We found our elders worshiping them' (Beliefs require blind following and not critical thinking.)  @ref{Quran 21:52-53}

This resistance persisted even in the face of divine revelations. Pharaoh and his followers, when confronted by Prophet Moses, dismissed his calls to reason, favoring the traditions of their ancestors:

@{q-trans-en} They said, 'Have you come to divert us (with your reasoning skills) from what we found our elders practicing? ...' @ref{Quran 10:78}

The same pattern recurred during the time of Prophet Muhammad. Those defending their ancestral beliefs dismissed rational inquiry, accusing the Prophet of fabricating lies to deviate them from their forefathers' traditions:

@{q-trans-en} When Our Ayaat (signs)  are conveyed to them in all clarity, they say, 'This is only a man who wants to divert you from what your elders used to worship.' and they (further) say, 'This is nothing but an invented lie.' ... @ref{Quran 34:43}

When invited to follow the rational guidance revealed in the Quran, they responded with unwavering loyalty to their ancestral ways:

@{q-trans-en}  When it is said to them, 'Follow what (rational guidance) God has revealed (in the Quran)', they say, 'Nay! We will follow the ways of our elders.' What! Even though their elders were lacking in understanding and were not rightly guided? @ref{Quran 2:170}

@{q-trans-en}  Rather, they say, "Indeed, we found our elders upon a religion, and we are in their footsteps [rightly] guided." @ref{Quran 43:22}

@{q-trans-en} ... they say, 'Enough for us is what we found our elders doing.' What! Even though their elders had no knowledge whatsoever and no guidance?' @ref{Quran 5:104}

> These Quranic verses vividly illustrate the persistent challenge of overcoming intellectual blindness and the unyielding grip of ancestral traditions. They serve as a timeless reminder of the importance of reason and critical thinking in the pursuit of truth and understanding.

## Herd Mentality

The Quran places supreme emphasis on the power of individual reasoning as the ultimate tool for discerning truth. It critiques those who blindly follow religious leaders without employing their intellect, likening them to sheep that obediently trail behind their shepherd. This behavior stems from habit and upbringing, neglecting the exercise of personal judgment and critical thinking.

@{q-trans-en} The likeness of truth-rejecters (due to blind following) is that (of a sheep) which hears the shepherd's call but hears in it nothing more than a sound and a shout (to be followed blindly). [Intellectually] Deaf, dumb, blind, for they do not use their reason [عقل]. @ref{Quran 2:171}

In another powerful metaphor, the Quran likens those who base their beliefs solely on ancestral traditions to individuals desiring eyes in the back of their heads — perpetually looking backward instead of critically examining the reality in front of them. This metaphor underscores the futility of anchoring one's convictions in the past while ignoring present truths.

@{q-trans-en} On the Day when their faces shall be tossed about in the fire (looking backward), they will say, 'Oh, We wish we had obeyed God, and obeyed the Messenger!' (who asked us to ponder over what was right in front of us.) .. And they will (further) say, 'Our Lord! We obeyed our revered clergy and the leaders and it is those who led us astray from the path .. Our Lord! Give them double punishment and banish them from Your grace.' @ref{Quran 33:66-68}

This stark imagery serves as a cautionary tale, urging individuals to rely on their intellectual faculties rather than succumbing to the allure of blind following or unquestioned loyalty to traditions. The Quran calls for a forward-looking perspective, rooted in reasoning and a quest for truth, to avoid the pitfalls of herd mentality.

## Religious Leaders and Their Followers

Religious leaders and scholars who mislead others through false teachings will face a double burden of accountability. They will not only bear the consequences of their own intellectual blindness but also the weight of leading others astray.

@{q-trans-en} They shall bear their burdens undiminished on Resurrection Day, with part of the burdens of those whom they misled ... Ah, evil is what they bear! @ref{Quran 16:25}

However, followers cannot escape their own responsibility. Blindly relying on religious leaders will not absolve them of the duty to use their reasoning and judgment. These followers, too, will face the consequences of their actions. In the depths of Hell — a state described in the Quran as both inner turmoil and societal chaos devoid of peace — they will engage in bitter debates with their leaders, filled with regret for their choices.

@{q-trans-en} ... If you could only see these transgressors when they are made to stand before their Lord, how they blame one another. The followers will tell their proud leaders, 'If it were not for you, we would have been believers.' @ref{Quran 34:31}

The Quran draws a stark comparison between those who neglect their intellectual faculties and animals, which require external restraints. Just as humans impose physical restraints on dangerous animals, God will impose shackles on those who lived without engaging their reason, behaving as if devoid of higher intellect.

@{q-trans-en} ... Both parties (religious leaders and their blind followers) will be filled with remorse when they see the doom. We will put shackles around the necks of all those who rejected the truth. Should they be rewarded with anything else except for what they used to do (i.e., behaving like animals)? @ref{Quran 34:33}

This vivid depiction serves as a powerful reminder of the critical importance of intellectual accountability. It emphasizes that each individual, regardless of their role or position, must actively seek truth through reason and discernment to avoid the perils of blind following and misguided leadership.
